### Material: Drainage Modeling Gaps and Water Management Research Needs

\subsection{Overview}

A striking feature of the subsurface (tile) drainage literature is that its
most confident statements are about what remains unknown. Across humid
temperate cropland, arid and semi-arid irrigated plains, saline coastal soils,
and tropical oases, authors converge on a shared diagnosis: theory, models,
and management hypotheses have outrun the field evidence available to test
them. Process representations remain untested outside the conditions in which
they were built; model parameters cannot be constrained without measurements
that were never made; and management practices are recommended on the strength
of short records at single sites. The research frontier is therefore defined
less by a shortage of ideas than by a shortage of the right data at the right
spatial and temporal resolution to calibrate, validate, and generalise. This
section organises the open questions, hypotheses, and methodological needs
that recur throughout the literature into a small set of interlocking themes.
First, process-based drainage and water-quality models (DRAINMOD and its
derivatives, SWAT, APEX, RZWQM2, HYDRUS, MIKE~SHE, CRHM, WEPP, and numerous
salinity codes) are repeatedly flagged as requiring rigorous independent
testing, with particular weaknesses in phosphorus, macropore and
preferential-flow, sediment, and seepage components
\parencite{Youssef2006,Askar2021b,Askar2025,Radcliffe2015,Williams2016}.
Second, the physical processes that most strongly govern drain
hydrographs---preferential and macropore flow, spatially and temporally
variable soil hydraulic properties, and the coupling among soil pore-water
flow, pipe flow, and surface water---remain incompletely characterised. Third,
the location, extent, and specifications of drainage networks are largely
unknown, motivating remote-sensing, geophysical, and database efforts. Fourth,
scaling from well-instrumented fields to the watersheds where water-quality
goals are set is an unresolved methodological problem. Fifth, active management
strategies---controlled drainage, subirrigation, drainage water recycling, and
edge-of-field treatment---lack the coordinated field-and-simulation programmes
needed to quantify their trade-offs. Sixth, salt leaching, drainage-water
reuse, and disposal remain only partially understood and only partially
observable. Finally, a persistent methodological agenda spans uncertainty
quantification, experimental design, long-term monitoring, economics, and the
institutional context of adoption. A cross-cutting message unites them: the
binding constraint is coordinated, long-term, year-round data, and progress
requires improved process representation and improved field verification to
advance together rather than separately
\parencite{Kladivko2001,Sharpley2019,Frankenberger2024,Schilling2008,Bauwe2016,Zhou2025}.

\subsection{Validating and extending process-based drainage models}

The most frequently cited need is more rigorous, independent testing of
simulation models beyond the conditions in which they were calibrated.
\textcite{Youssef2006} describe rigorous field testing across differing
management, soils, and climates as a need common to nearly all simulation
models, and warn that model assumptions must be re-verified whenever a code is
transferred to a new area \parencite{Kelleners2000}. Similar generic calls for
broader-condition testing, sensitivity analysis, and parameter refinement recur
for pesticide, salt, and flow models
\parencite{Branger2009,Hirekhan2007,Yang2008b,Xian2017}. \textcite{Yang2008b}
stress that soil coefficients---particularly the
drainage-volume/water-table-depth relationship---must be locally adjusted and
propose integrating remote sensing and GIS into decision-support tools;
\textcite{Xian2017} seek alternate approaches to simulate drainage peaks; and
\textcite{Mendez2004} argue that long simulations of 50--100 years are needed to
judge whether a drainage design meets its criteria, a horizon echoed for design
evaluation generally \parencite{Wang2006b,Ale2009,Malota2015,Häggblom2019}.

\subsubsection{DRAINMOD, controlled drainage, and drainage water recycling components}

Extensions of DRAINMOD are singled out for continued development.
\textcite{Askar2021b} call for testing newly incorporated
macropore-flow, phosphorus-cycle, and sediment components under varying water
management, farming practice, and climate, and \textcite{Askar2025} extend this
to the phosphorus and soil-erosion components, recommending that key
coefficients become user inputs. For drainage water management and recycling,
\textcite{Moursi2022} call for testing DRAINMOD-DWR against field data and for
routines simulating the fate of nitrogen, phosphorus, and sediment in the
storage reservoir, while \textcite{Moursi2023} add that the reservoir's
physical, chemical, and biological processes must be investigated to optimise
treatment and to identify when reservoirs become nutrient sources; DRAINMOD-DWR
is likewise needed to aid recycling-system design under varied weather, soil,
and design conditions with economic optimisation \parencite{Moursi2024}. Related
model-development needs include a time-variable surface-depression capacity and
wastewater/urban modules \parencite{Abduljaleel2023}, evaluation of a
DRAINMOD-ANN model across soil textures \parencite{Awad2022}, and complementary
$K_s$-measurement relationships and evaluation methods in data-scarce settings
\parencite{NabiSichani2012,Salazar2008}. \textcite{Youssef2018} outline the
experimental verification required for controlled drainage---side-by-side
controlled and free plots with extensive root-zone, shallow-groundwater, and
outflow measurements sustained across seasons, plus a separate seepage
simulation---and \textcite{Skaggs2010} stress that such experiments must be run
at field or watershed scale so that seepage is properly represented, while
\textcite{Skaggs2006} note that simplified design methods require testing across
wider drain depths and surface storages before general acceptance.

\subsubsection{Phosphorus, sediment, and nutrient routines in water-quality models}

Phosphorus and its transport through macropores emerge as the least mature
component across water-quality models. \textcite{Radcliffe2015} provide the most
comprehensive agenda: nested paired field experiments to improve P models for
drained fields, and structural additions across codes---SWAT and ADAPT should
route macropore flow directly to drains, APEX needs a macropore component, HSPF
and P-indices should simulate P movement to ditches or drains, DRAINMOD should
simulate P losses to drains and sediment-attached P in runoff, PLEASE needs a
runoff component, and HYDRUS could parameterise simpler models---with every
study quantifying predicted-P accuracy and uncertainty. Consistent with this,
\textcite{Guo2020b} recommend physically based tile-drainage subroutines,
macropore nutrient pathways, particulate-P losses through tiles, and
soil-test-P effects in the APEX/NTT interface; \textcite{Ford2017} seek testing
under varied fertiliser management and better understanding of how macropore
flow and P respond to tillage, cover crops, and drainage water management; and
\textcite{BinShokrana2023b} identify improved fertiliser-P partitioning,
macropore-flow simulation, and P-only performance statistics for a DRAINMOD P
component, with \textcite{Gowda2012} anticipating a DRAINMOD phosphorus module.
Within SWAT, APEX, and RZWQM2, \textcite{Williams2016} argue that incorporating
identified flow pathways is critical for P estimates, \textcite{Qi2018}
recommend a soil-matrix-potential Richards equation and revisiting the
particulate-P loss algorithm, and \textcite{Moriasi2012} call for comparing
dynamic and constant surface-depression storage. Supporting empirical work is
required: further testing of CaCl$_2$-P as a dissolved-reactive-phosphorus
estimator across soils and rainfall regimes \parencite{McDowell2001b}, field
research on how drainage parameters affect P losses with improved regression
models \parencite{Morrison2013}, tests using actual drainage effluent under
fluctuating flow \parencite{Sanford2016}, and long-term monitoring of P sources
and transport \parencite{Jia2012}. Nitrogen-cycle needs include surface-residue
and mobile/immobile-water phases for nitrate transport \parencite{Malone2017},
more empirical data on soil-water/soil-N interactions and complete N budgets in
calibration \parencite{Gillette2018}, and deterministic land-use and temporal
decay-coefficient representations \parencite{Rudra2005}.

\subsubsection{Salinity, salt balance, and unsaturated-zone coupling}

In arid and irrigated settings, needs cluster around salt-transport validation
and tighter coupling of saturated and unsaturated processes.
\textcite{Ebrahimian2015} recommend models simulating salt and nitrate in
drainage water and the soil profile, while \textcite{Sarangi2006} note that
neural networks predict effluent salinity from minimal input but that SALTMOD
may be preferred for detailed salt balances, and \textcite{Hirekhan2007} caution
that more evaluation precedes firm salt-transport recommendations. Several
Chinese studies press for coupling water and salt dynamics with unsaturated
models such as HYDRUS-1D/2D, SWAP, and UBMOD-S, regional layout parameters, and
multi-objective optimisation under climatic, textural, and groundwater
variability \parencite{Han2025,Wang2025c,Wang2024c}. \textcite{Liu2022} call for
a root-growth module, automatic TDR monitoring, and large-dataset validation,
and \textcite{Liu2026} for climate-change scenarios, deep-soil salt
accumulation, and real-world scalability; \textcite{Guo2025} seek longer
observations, optimal leaching quotas, and cost-benefit analyses. Earlier
salinity series similarly staged validation and salt-flow analysis across
successive papers \parencite{KARAJEH1994}, and \textcite{Kelleners2000} warn
that proper regional-seepage representation would demand a fundamentally
different modelling approach.

\subsection{Preferential and macropore flow: characterisation and modelling}

The representation of preferential flow is perhaps the most frequently cited
frontier, and the recurring theme is that its contribution to drains must be
characterised as a function of soil type, water content, and agricultural
practice before models can be extrapolated. \textcite{Gaillot2023} and
\textcite{Fox2004} call for more mechanistic characterisation of
macropore--drain connectivity and of the ``express fraction'' under varying
saturated--unsaturated conditions above the drain, with testing across climate,
soil, drain configuration, and chemical application. \textcite{Askar2020} stress
that rigorous testing of DRAINMOD's empirical macropore approach requires
water-table-depth measurements alongside drainage and runoff, direct
macropore-flow inputs, and detailed crack geometry and temporal crack dynamics.
Process improvements are proposed across frameworks: better matrix--macropore
water partitioning and calibration against sub-hourly drainage in RZWQM2
\parencite{Li2023d}; dynamic macropore connectivity or effective macroporosity
and a soil-moisture transfer factor at watershed scale \parencite{Poon2021};
micropore diffusion and additional domains, with site-specific matrix and
macropore conductivity, in dual-permeability descriptions
\parencite{Villholth1998,Villholth1998b}; and hysteresis and biopore-flow
initiation validated across the profile \parencite{Holbak2021}. Structural
controls that models neglect include manure-driven macropore development
\parencite{Zhao2001}, crack re-establishment and nutrient transfer in EPIC
\parencite{Wang2022}, and calibrated macropore conductivity within a
three-dimensional dual-permeability model \parencite{Varvaris2018}. Direct
identification of pathways is repeatedly urged---ground-penetrating radar and
tracers with explicit flowpath and open-ditch representation
\parencite{Tähtikarhu2025}, tracer-tracked routes with solute-transport
simulation \parencite{Salo2017}, dye-staining of pathways reaching tile lines
\parencite{Nielsen2015}, and characterisation of near-surface pathways across
settings \parencite{Frey2012,Allaire2011}. Central quantitative questions
remain the fraction of phosphorus and other solutes delivered via preferential
versus matrix flow \parencite{King2015,Williams2016}, the range of macropore
flows to drains across soils and management \parencite{Reid2012}, whether
vertical macropore transport occurs in deep saturated layers
\parencite{Villholth1998}, and by-pass flow through cracks for full-year model
application \parencite{LESAFFRE1988,BACKLUND1995,Ruark2009}.

\subsection{Coupled hydraulics, seepage, and soil heterogeneity}

\subsubsection{Coupled surface--subsurface, pipe-flow, and open-ditch hydraulics}

A recurrent structural gap is the absence of models that couple soil pore-water
flow with surface flow inside drains and ditches. \textcite{Xin2016b} call for
coupling drain surface water with soil pore-water flow to represent the
time-varying head around the drain and for extending analytical solutions to
rainfall and evapotranspiration; \textcite{Nousiainen2015} propose simulating
in-pipe flow to capture pressurisation and clogging, adding open-ditch networks
and crop-specific evapotranspiration. Pressurised-flow dynamics are a specific
frontier: coupling pipes to the water table could introduce a validated
temporary-retention function beyond design capacity
\parencite{Henine2010,Henine2014}, and pressurised periods should be analysed
separately using pressure-transducer data \parencite{Saadat2018}. Entrance
resistance and near-drain representation remain unresolved for old and
heterogeneous systems, motivating higher near-drain pressure heads, finer
elements, and questions about rapid trench-backfill versus slower inter-trench
transport \parencite{Messing2006,Rimidis2003}. Model dimensionality is itself a
research question---two- or three-dimensional approaches for sloping fields and
flow partitioning \parencite{Alakukku2010,FIPPS1989}, better collector and
extreme-parameter representation \parencite{Carlier2007}, and three-dimensional
treatment of regional water and chemical management
\parencite{Mohanty1998}---and broader integrating frameworks are sought within
SWAT and WEPP \parencite{Manguerra1998,SAVABI1993,RevueltaAcosta2021}.

\subsubsection{Spatial and temporal heterogeneity of soil hydraulic properties}

Nearly every modelling study attributes residual error to unresolved
heterogeneity in hydraulic properties, especially conductivity. Authors call for
more detailed representation of macropores, fractures, and sand lenses, with
specific storage and residual saturation in calibration
\parencite{DeSchepper2017}, for depth-varying and dual-porosity parameterisations
in layered and paddy soils \parencite{Wesström2003,Karandish2018}, and for
denser, depth-resolved sampling---at least one conductivity measurement per
hectare with post-construction verification \parencite{PalaciosVélez2004}, more
transect points to capture spatial variability \parencite{Messing2006}, and
horizontal as well as vertical conductivities with hydrophobicity and hysteresis
\parencite{Tähtikarhu2025}. Anisotropy and long-term property change form a
related sub-frontier \parencite{Akis2015,Moustafa1998,Koivusalo1999}, and
accurate saturated conductivity and drainage-volume/upward-flux relationships,
supported by comprehensive soil survey and SSURGO-linked subsoil porosity, are
emphasised as DRAINMOD and watershed-model inputs
\parencite{DeBoer2006,Schilling2015}. Larger-scale models representing
heterogeneous clayey tills in greater detail are viewed as a cornerstone of
future nutrient-transport modelling \parencite{DeSchepper2015}.

\subsubsection{Seepage, groundwater exchange, and inverse calibration}

Closing the water balance---particularly seepage and groundwater exchange---
remains a persistent gap. Lateral seepage should be allowed to vary during
simulation and included among inputs \parencite{Feng2018}, groundwater
contributions could enter through double-storage or residual-storage models
given hydrochemical data \parencite{DaneshYazdi2016}, and providing
groundwater-table data in objective functions would sharpen inverse recovery of
soil properties \parencite{Sedaghatdoost2018,Sedaghatdoost2019}. Improved
calibration strategy is widely urged---parameter-estimation software and
multiple conceptual models \parencite{Boico2022}, pilot-point distributed
conductivity fields with soil-moisture observations and proxy machine-learning
predictions \parencite{Schneider2022}, alternative objective functions such as
Kling--Gupta efficiency \parencite{Brighenti2023}, and combined
catchment--drainage-discharge calibration \parencite{DeSchepper2017}. Regional
groundwater assessments call for radiocarbon sampling and recharge-zone chemistry
\parencite{Tweed2018,Dragon2016}, deep-percolation analysis and aquifer-layer
characterisation \parencite{BOOKMAN1959,KAMRA1991,KAMRA1994}, greater use of
test wells with attention to biofouling \parencite{Stuetz2004}, transient tests
of steady-state assumptions \parencite{Schilling2022}, and unsteady flow and
mass-transport models with stochastic analysis at watershed scale
\parencite{Gates2002,Burkhalter2006}. Contemporary needs also span unsaturated
and preferential-flow modules for disconnected groundwater
\parencite{Kompanizare2024}, independent methods for layer-to-layer drainage
under preferential flow \parencite{Ojeda2018}, dynamic frozen-soil and
air-temperature models with measured soil structure and P \parencite{Ulén2016},
and machine-learning surrogates trained across diverse climatic and
hydrogeological conditions with transient processes and real-time control
\parencite{Lei2026}.

\subsection{From field to watershed: scaling, routing, and connectivity}

A defining open problem is the disconnect between field-scale process knowledge
and watershed-scale response. Quantifying the relationships between field
processes and watershed response is repeatedly identified as a critical need for
water-quality strategy, with explicit field-to-watershed linkage and upscaling
frameworks \parencite{Williams2015,Williams2017,Wang2020b}. Small, intensively
monitored catchments are proposed as the empirical bridge for upscaling and
model validation \parencite{Kyllmar2023,Basu2010}, and effectiveness estimates
for drainage water management and phosphorus mitigation should be scaled up from
field to watershed \parencite{Wang2021,Ross2016,Shedekar2021}. The hydrologic
effects of tiling remain inadequately characterised at watershed scale and must
be pursued through combined modelling and field data---attending to seasonal
streamflow recessions in tiled versus non-tiled watersheds
\parencite{Schilling2008,Schilling2012}, events of all sizes across landscapes
\parencite{Sloan2017}, routing between spatially variable depressional stores and
flow accumulation across river networks \parencite{Sloan2016,Fonley2021}, and the
decoupling of row-crop cover from artificial drainage and of surface from
subsurface contributions \parencite{BolandBrien2014}. Interactions between drains
and the receiving channel network are a frontier in their own right: the fate and
transport of tile effluent after it reaches surface drains
\parencite{Clement2017}, drain--river/ditch interactions \parencite{Grangeon2021},
and field-scale monitoring of tile flow's impact on downstream discharge, stream
power, and channel geomorphology \parencite{Zhou2025,Zhou2013}. Streamflow
prediction in tiled landscapes remains difficult, raising the question of whether
curve-number or more process-based methods are required
\parencite{Wessel2022,Thomas2016,Rahman2014}.

Channel-network, in-stream, and sediment processes are underdeveloped in
watershed models: networks must be modelled as interdependent reaches with flow
paths and travel times \parencite{Giraud1997,Williamson2025}, instream
biogeochemistry and hillslope--riparian interaction need stronger representation
\parencite{Schilling2012b,Basu2010}, and gully, streambank, and non-field
sediment sources require mechanistic treatment and catchment-scale sediment
budgets \parencite{Maalim2013,Dalzell2018,Walling2002}. Connectivity metrics are
flagged as management-relevant: mapping surface ditches may be more efficient than
locating in-field drains, and future P-risk models should include ditch
connectivity and lag times \parencite{Moloney2020,Opoku2024}, with improved
connectivity algorithms and monitoring-based thresholds for Mediterranean lowland
catchments \parencite{Calsamiglia2018}. Drained closed depressions and potholes
are singled out as poorly understood, demanding process study at additional
locations, quantification of runoff/tile/antecedent-moisture relationships, and
data on blind-inlet longevity \parencite{Martin2019,Williams2020,Feyereisen2015b}.
Newly proposed physically based tools such as the Advanced Terrestrial Simulator
and HYDRUS-2D warrant scaling and transferability tests across soils,
topography, land-use change, and climate
\parencite{Rathore2025,Naderi2025,Basu2010}.

\subsection{Locating, mapping, and cataloguing drainage networks}

Because the physical drainage network is often unknown, uncertainty propagates
into every downstream model, making detection and mapping a fast-moving frontier
where methods work in some conditions but lack systematic guidelines. Actual tile
locations and measured discharge would improve calibration
\parencite{Amado2018}, and predictive mapping from imagery needs validation
against GPS surveys and methods that avoid misidentifying noise and connect broken
segments \parencite{Naz2009,Schilling2019}. The most explicit data agenda calls
for reporting tile diameter, depth, spacing, and mains, and for a drainage
database built jointly by agencies, researchers, landowners, and contractors,
with repositories updated as data accrue
\parencite{Zhou2025,Valayamkunnath2020}. Remote-sensing work seeks urgent
development of non-invasive methods for aging systems, combining UAV flights with
GPR \parencite{Wienken2024}, and multispectral, thermal-infrared, and
machine-learning agendas that define the site conditions under which drain lines
and outlets can be mapped, with decision-tree guidelines for practitioners
\parencite{Allred2020,Allred2021,Allred2022}. \textcite{Carlsen2024} recommend
reconsidering the often-cited 2.54~cm minimum rainfall for imagery acquisition,
investigating deep learning, multitemporal acquisition, and synthetic aperture
radar, and expanding public drainage-map datasets, echoed by calls for
transformer-based architectures and higher-resolution imagery integrated into
hydrologic frameworks \parencite{Wan2024}, and for reduced tracing uncertainty
through multiple interpreters, ground truth, and richer covariates
\parencite{Redoloza2023,Harris2018,Yimer2023}. Geophysical evaluations seek data
from more sites and soils \parencite{Allred2005}, systematic testing of GPR
antenna configuration and orientation \parencite{Allred2013}, prediction of GPR
suitability via electromagnetic simulation from EMI, and concurrent EMI/GPR and
drone/magnetic surveys with dependence on soil, residue, tillage, and wetness
\parencite{Koganti2020,Koganti2021,Jouen2018,Kratt2020,Allred2018b}. Optical and
radar covariate studies also seek more frequent imaging, repeated SAR with
ground measurements, and UAV cover-crop mapping
\parencite{Tlapáková2017,Meunier2001,Xu2018}.

\subsection{Long-term, year-round, and multi-scale monitoring}

Across nutrient, salt, and hydrological studies, authors emphasise that short,
seasonally incomplete records mislead. Year-round measurement of flow and
concentration, for at least several years on new sites, is deemed necessary to set
management priorities and to partition tile flow from surface runoff at watershed
scale \parencite{Kladivko2001}. \textcite{Sharpley2019} present a broad agenda:
closing water and nutrient balances, studying legacy N and P pools,
quantifying snowmelt-driven losses, and evaluating single, stacked, and
edge-of-field practices. \textcite{Frankenberger2024} detail monitoring
recommendations for drainage water management---high-frequency discharge, runoff,
and seepage to close the water budget; process measurements of microbial activity,
denitrification, soil-P pools, and redox; consistent documentation of design and
management; and a publicly available dataset. Long-term records are singled out as
necessary to disentangle land-management from climate-change effects, with the
corollary that policy evaluation demands patience
\parencite{Bauwe2020,Kelley2017,Gillingham2000,Lemke2011}. Long-term monitoring is
likewise sought for edge-of-field and treatment systems---biofouling of
recycled-concrete-aggregate drains \parencite{McCulloch2017}, maturation of
constructed wetlands for P retention \parencite{Mendes2020}, stormwater-retention
devices \parencite{Xu2022}, and cumulative groundwater-level effects
\parencite{Okuda2020}---and for tracking soil physical, biological, and carbon
properties over time \parencite{Frankl2023,Furukawa2008}. Tracer and water-age
studies add specific demands: high-frequency tracer measurements to quantify water
ages and represent both celerities and velocities \parencite{Williams2021},
event sampling and bulk-soil-water isotopes to constrain young-water fractions
\parencite{DimitrovaPetrova2020}, high-resolution specific conductance for
end-member mixing \parencite{Kronholm2015,Schilling2019}, and water-isotope or
conservative-tracer studies to partition quick flow and baseflow
\parencite{Frederiksen2023}. National drainage-extent datasets open new analyses
but require clarification of how farmers report ambiguous cases
\parencite{Jame2022}, and coordinated international collaboration with consistent
reporting of basic performance parameters is urged for treatment systems
\parencite{Messer2021,Sims1998}.

\subsection{Transferability across soils, climates, crops, and management}

A pervasive limitation is that findings from single sites or narrow conditions
cannot yet be generalised. \textcite{Singh2022b} recommend that
controlled-drainage and subirrigation studies be conducted outside the U.S.\
Midwest and Canada across diverse climates, crops, and soils, and that site
characterisation---slope, particle-size distribution by horizon, hydraulic
conductivity, drainage class, and moisture---be reported with measures of
variability. Repetition at larger scales and on different soils is stressed as a
key modulating factor \parencite{Bilotta2008,Goeken2015}, including earthworm
studies on soils of different texture and rainfall \parencite{Nuutinen2001,Nuutinen2011}.
Climate-focused transferability needs include high-resolution climate-change
scenarios \parencite{Rasmussen2018}, multi-model uncertainty analysis at
additional sites \parencite{Jeantet2022}, and drainage-warming and soil-texture
effects on crops \parencite{Jin2008}. Crop- and practice-specific extensions are
sought for paddy and trench drainage \parencite{Rahimi2024,Jia2023}, mole-drainage
durability \parencite{JHA1995}, controlled-drainage principles for new regions
\parencite{Sojka2020}, crop-rotation and tillage interactions
\parencite{Ojeda2018}, and salt-flushing methods in heterogeneous soils
\parencite{Yu2018}. Additional testing across regions, soils, and climates recurs
throughout \parencite{Singh2020b,Malota2024,Salazar2008,Holbak2021}, and localised
design methods reflecting local agro-technical conditions are called for
\parencite{Tlapáková2017,Sinai2006,He2002}.

\subsection{Controlled drainage, subirrigation, and drainage water management}

Integrating controlled drainage (CD) with irrigation is repeatedly framed less as
settled practice than as a research frontier: the recurring call is to move from
passive, one-way ``unilateral'' drainage toward active, location-specific control
of the water table and the coupled irrigation--drainage balance
\parencite{Ayars2006b,Kesicka2023b,Ritzema2023}. \textcite{Ayars2006b} argue that
water-table-control structures should be required in new designs and considered
for retrofits, and that new criteria and management methods are needed---claims
requiring long-term field studies, possibly combined with modelling, to
validate---echoed by calls to build in control options and expand changeable CD
networks \parencite{Dukhovny2018,Mahmoud2021,Strock2011}. The quantitative payoff
of active management remains open: \textcite{Watkins2024} hypothesise that active
outlet management during extremely wet periods could minimise wet-stress and raise
yields, \textcite{Saadat2017} emphasise the cost--benefit trade-off, and
\textcite{Helmers2012} suggest CD yield reductions could be mitigated by greater
management while weighing installation cost, management burden, and applicable
acreage. Seasonally differentiated control---free drainage in wet seasons, CD in
dry seasons, with supplemental irrigation for extremes---is an explicit hypothesis
\parencite{Asgari2021}.

Automation and data-driven control are identified as the next technological step.
\textcite{Youssef2023} call for a new generation of control structures and
decision-support tools, sensor-based systems for shallow-water-table soils, and
systems that adapt to changing weather, and \textcite{Ayars2015} for
adaptive-control decision support. Concrete agendas include dynamic modelling and
data assimilation of the field water balance, feasible control-problem
formulations, and fusion of control automation with physical infrastructure
\parencite{Ikonen2023,Isomäki2024}, automatic water-depth control for aerobic rice
\parencite{Arif2021}, and earlier farm decision aids incorporating quantity,
quality, crop growth, and economics \parencite{THOMAS1995}. Coordinating
irrigation with drainage control is a persistent problem: irrigation must be
reduced when CD is implemented, requiring coordination among authorities and
farmers \parencite{Wahba2005,Ritzema2011,Ritzema2016}, and CD requires genuinely
integrated management with careful site selection \parencite{Dukhovny2018}.
Subirrigation embedded in regional water systems is a distinct, largely
unresolved frontier: regional water needs should be known before responsible
incorporation, with optimised pumping and cautious use of alternative resources
\parencite{deWit2022}, and SWAP-type modelling can pre-screen sites and forecast
consequences before implementation \parencite{deWit2024}. Seepage effects on CD
performance need coordinated experimental and modelling research, including guard
lines to evaluate lateral seepage \parencite{Youssef2021,Pease2017,Ale2009}, and
excess-moisture stress, spring drawdown, and spring nitrogen loading are priorities
\parencite{Craft2018,Lahdou2019}. Anticipatory, forecast-based management should
incorporate evapotranspiration, subirrigation, and spatially variable seepage and
address forecast uncertainty \parencite{VandeCraats2021}, collector-ditch
water-level and control schedules should be optimised
\parencite{Salla2022,Strock2025b}, and CD should be paired with local crop and
climate characteristics without overlooking rapid water-table decrease raising
irrigation demand in dry summers
\parencite{Güngör2023,Dou2023,Golmohammadi2016,Kim2023,Wang2020c}. Longer-term
studies must disentangle whether soil-property changes under
drainage--subirrigation arise from the practice or from irrigation-water quality
\parencite{Kaur2023}, and complete water and nutrient budgets within and across
field boundaries, plus effects on runoff, preferential flow, and recharge, remain
to be tracked \parencite{Shedekar2021,Ross2016,King2022}.

\subsection{Novel structures, design criteria, and drainage intensity}

Novel drainage structures and materials define a further set of open questions:
improved HYDRUS-2D boundaries and field verification of siphon drainage
\parencite{Ren2023}, long-term measurement of working time versus construction cost
\parencite{Tao2025b}, and new structures that adjust water-holding capacity while
inhibiting rapid vertical and bypass flow \parencite{Li2021}. Multi-level and
bi-level configurations warrant study of actively managed shallow and deep drains
\parencite{Hornbuckle2007,Verma1998,Nelson2011b}, and envelope, perforation, and
screen design remain open across gradients, soils, and textures
\parencite{Alavi2021,Wu2022,Oyarce2017,HajAmor2019}. Optimal layout and
burial-depth determination in saline and problem soils recur in recent Chinese
studies, stressing ditching depth, restrictive-layer breaking, amendments, and
construction cost within multi-scheme decision models
\parencite{Tian2023,Tian2024,Tian2023c,Zhang2024d,Zhai2023,Han2023,Qian2025b,Ren2021,Zheng2025,SerranoCoronel2018,Zegeye2021}.
Analytical and theoretical advances---formulas for sloped and multilayer soils
\parencite{Tao2016,Tao2019}, partially penetrating and trapezoidal ditches
\parencite{Chahar2010,Afruzi2014}, and mass-transfer and three-dimensional flow
effects \parencite{Zhang2024}---are also sought, and CAD/GIS-based design software
and standardised drainage-coefficient definitions are recommended to enable
comparison \parencite{GALLICHAND1992b,Skaggs2017}.

Many site studies conclude that established design criteria require re-evaluation.
New criteria protecting crops while minimising drain-water salinity and volume are
sought \parencite{Christen2001}, along with updated depths and spacings at specific
sites \parencite{Muhammad2021,Malota2016,Bahçeci2009,Kahlown2002}, review of
recommended spacings paired with controlled-traffic farming
\parencite{Mante2022,Mante2018}, cost-effectiveness of narrow spacings in claypan
soils \parencite{Nelson2017}, and standard depth-and-spacing guidelines tested
in situ before extension \parencite{HajAmor2019}. Design should account for
changing crop water needs, climate, and irrigation modernisation
\parencite{Rui2024,Akram2013,Ziaeifar2025,Ritzema2023,Ritzema2007,Ritzema2006,Ritzema2011},
and design intensity itself is an open question: reduced spacing would require
increased network capacity and heighten reliance on best management practices
\parencite{Singh2009,Dialameh2025}, a systems approach should integrate drainage,
crop--soil processes, and nutrient-loss reduction \parencite{Castellano2019}, and
DRAINMOD-based analysis should develop optimum rates and spacings across locations
\parencite{Strock2011}. Which soil parameters are most critical, and what
confidence intervals each requires, remain fundamental questions
\parencite{Ayars2015,SHIRMOHAMMADI1995,HARDJOAMIDJOJO1982}. Design guidance must
rest on long records: drainage design should not rely on short-term simulations
\parencite{Wang2006b}, and long runs with hourly input are needed to assess wider
spacings, interannual variability, and shallow-water-table control
\parencite{Ale2009,Häggblom2019,Malota2015,Salo2019,Kladivko2023,Chang2012}.

\subsection{Salt leaching, drainage-water reuse, and disposal}

Subsurface drainage is a cornerstone for reclaiming salt-affected land, yet its
salt-leaching outcomes are only partially understood and only partially detectable
with current programs. The recurring message is temporal: meaningful salt
reduction often accrues slowly, so short windows underestimate long-term benefits,
and only extended drainage with sustained monitoring resolves the true trajectory
\parencite{Almen2021,Daigh2025}. Long-term effects of combined saline-water
irrigation and subsurface drainage on salinity and yield require further evaluation
\parencite{Feng2017,Feng2021}, desalination rates and trends remain incompletely
quantified \parencite{Guo2024b,Yao2024}, and controlled drainage introduces
unresolved temporal trade-offs for salt accumulation
\parencite{Jouni2018,Lu2019,Islam2025,Heng2021}. Better measurement of what leaves
through the drains is essential: greater attention to surface-water monitoring of
salt and fertiliser export \parencite{Mastrocicco2013}, continuous flow meters and
electrical-conductivity probes for oasis-scale balances with evaluation of impacts
on downstream phreatic groundwater \parencite{Askri2022}, nutrient balances of
recipient waters \parencite{Turtola1995}, and cluster wells and deep sampling to
track salinity over time \parencite{Singh2020d,Gafni2001}.

Process understanding of salt mobilisation and removal remains an active frontier:
how macropores affect transient-state leaching and how methods might minimise those
effects \parencite{Xin2016}, governing factors in low-permeability and structured
media \parencite{Zhang2022}, salt-replacement between pipes under brackish-water
leaching \parencite{Li2023}, rainfall/irrigation interactions and generalisable
desalinization ratios across climates and soils
\parencite{Yu2015,Chen2025,Wang2024d}, ponding strategies and leaching curves
\parencite{RAO1991,Yang2022c}, and optimal root-zone matric potential linking
leaching to crop response \parencite{Zhang2021,Heng2021}. Sensing and modelling of
salinity is a large cluster: EMI mapping validated across soil orders and settings
\parencite{Narjary2024}, coupled apparent-conductivity--water-content models
\parencite{Yu2025}, EM38 and multispectral/hyperspectral remote sensing
\parencite{Singh2020d}, model interfaces incorporating tile drains
\parencite{Singh2020d,Jayawardane2001}, and an integrative vision of zonal design
algorithms with a multi-scale water-and-salt transport model coupled to
remote-sensing inversion for real-time early warning \parencite{Zhu2025b}.
Design under heterogeneity requires integrated redesign of system, surface-water,
and nutrient management \parencite{vandenEertwegh2006}, texture-specific
understanding of preferential subsurface drainage economics \parencite{Neha2022},
and vertical porous flow pipes or fully charged segments to enhance salt drainage
\parencite{Wang2024d,Yao2024,Jayawardane2001}.

Removing salt from soil relocates the problem to the drainage water, whose
management is among the most persistent challenges. Environmentally acceptable
disposal methods are needed \parencite{Ayars2014,Steppuhn2006}, evaporation-pond
management must be tested at full scale and combined with volume/contamination
reduction \parencite{BRADFORD1991}, and trace-element fate---arsenic speciation and
uranium recovery or removal---compounds the problem
\parencite{Gao2007,Duff1997,Gao2003}. Emerging treatment routes are at the
demonstration stage: electrolysis of saline wastewater at varying salinity
\parencite{Yang2023}, sorptive filter materials tested with real rather than
artificial drainage water \parencite{Pugliese2023}, and blending of drainage and
reverse-osmosis product water \parencite{Singh2020d}. Reuse-focused research spans
site-specific load--flow relationships, germination-stage salinity tolerance, and
improved scheduling that meets crop evapotranspiration from a shallow water table
while minimising upward salt flow \parencite{WESTCOT1988}, provisional field
guidance on substituting drainage water for wheat \parencite{Sharma1998}, blending
and evaporation-pond storage \parencite{Datta2002}, farm-level reuse within a
basin-wide salt balance \parencite{Qureshi2008}, seasonal groundwater undulation
and drip conversion to prevent accumulation \parencite{Mirlas2021,Dou2022,Singh2019},
and biochar, phytoremediation, and reclamation innovations
\parencite{Liu2025f,Steppuhn2017,Youngs2000}. Comprehensive assessment of
waterlogging and salinity together, rather than separately, is recommended
\parencite{Valipour2014}, and the economic feasibility of drainage as a preventive
measure against salinisation remains to be established
\parencite{Datta2000,HalbacCotoaraZamfir2022}.

\subsection{Nutrient, carbon, and greenhouse-gas coupling}

Improving the representation and understanding of nutrient transport---especially
phosphorus---is a persistent priority. \textcite{King2015} call for enhanced
phosphorus routines and simultaneous measurement of P losses in surface runoff and
subsurface drainage to determine partitioning and avoid unintended consequences,
and \textcite{Williams2016} stress differentiating soil versus fertiliser sources
of tile-drained P. Additional needs include measuring the size distribution of
particulate-P carriers \parencite{Qi2018}, determining which processes most reduce
total-P loadings to drains \parencite{Geohring2001}, and retaining P at drain
outlets \parencite{Zimmer2016}. Broader questions extend to nitrogen and
receiving waters: P retention and uptake lengths and internal lake P loading
\parencite{Clement2017}, individual and stacked practices to mitigate algal blooms
\parencite{King2018}, cover crops, drainage water management, and 4R nutrient
management targeting the non-growing season
\parencite{King2016,Kokulan2021}, and legacy soil P interacting with hydrology in
drained landscapes and closed depressions
\parencite{Mumbi2024,Mumbi2025b,Hanrahan2020}. At watershed scale, controlling
surface runoff and tile flow with drainage-water-management devices and
bioreactors, and strategies addressing both hydrology and nitrogen supply at
district scale, are argued necessary for downstream benefit
\parencite{Li2015,Ikenberry2014,Filipovic2015,BinShokrana2023,Koskiaho2002,Kladivko2005}.
The full water-quality potential of controlled drainage and water-table
management---across chemical fate, soil properties, and biological dynamics, plus a
comprehensive solute-transport model---remains to be evaluated
\parencite{FAUSEY1995,RITTER1995}, and dissolved-organic-carbon dynamics and their
ecological implications are largely unknown \parencite{Ruark2009,Furukawa2008}.

Greenhouse-gas coupling is a notable gap. Fluxes---carbon sequestration,
pre-implementation emissions, and dissolved nitrous oxide in tile drainage and
conservation-practice outflows---need quantification \parencite{Mitchell2023}, and
coordinated large-scale studies must constrain the climate-mitigation potential of
conservation drainage, few of which have simultaneously measured N\textsubscript{2}O,
CH\textsubscript{4}, and soil-organic-carbon change at the same sites
\parencite{Hall2025}. Longer-term dynamics of wetland carbon storage,
N\textsubscript{2}O, and CH\textsubscript{4}, and the greenhouse-gas effects of
upgrading drainage infrastructure, remain unresolved \parencite{Mitchell2022}, and
carbon budgets should be integrated into wetland-design models \parencite{Kadlec2012}.
Dissolved-organic-matter quality, microbial availability, and episodic
drainage/precipitation influence demand paired-watershed designs and
scale-appropriate sampling \parencite{Dalzell2011,Bellmore2015}.

\subsection{Practice-scale treatment: wetlands, buffers, bioreactors, and recycling}

Detailed process modelling of individual conservation practices is a recurring
need. Hourly-time-step dynamic modelling is required to approximate nitrate removal
in tile-fed wetlands, with means to buffer and gradually release flows
\parencite{Tanner2011}; constructed-wetland siting and sizing depend on
wetland-to-catchment ratio and seasonal discharge, requiring long-term study of
sustainable nitrogen-retention efficiency, N\textsubscript{2}O emission processes,
and pesticide-metabolite accumulation
\parencite{Steidl2019,Tournebize2017,Kovacic2006,Haverstock2017}. Passing
tile-drained water through wetlands is a leading nitrogen-load strategy motivating
basin-scale siting analyses \parencite{Baeumler2020,Billy2013,Madramootoo2007,Kadlec2012}.
Edge-of-field systems present gaps in surface/subsurface nitrogen removal and
phosphorus-plant harvest \parencite{Gordon2021}, verification of design retention
times and hydraulic conductivity in bioreactors \parencite{Feyereisen2015}, and
placement of drainage beneath vegetative filter strips \parencite{Bhattarai2009};
phosphorus-sorbing filters need testing at higher loadings in both pathways, and
filter and grass strips need dormant-season and harvest evaluation
\parencite{Plach2023,Holly2016,Vallée2015}. For drainage water recycling,
long-term moisture-sensor and automated monitoring, DRAINMOD-DWR-aided design,
and economic optimisation are sought \parencite{Kaur2021,Moursi2024,Hay2021,Willison2021},
alongside regional economic-benefit and reservoir-sizing analyses under future
climate \parencite{Kosari2024,Li2021b}. Whether drainage water management within
channelized streams can restore natural hydrology, and how it affects headwater
biogeochemistry, requires integrated hydrology--water-quality--ecology assessment
\parencite{King2009,Sunohara2015,Dalzell2018}, and multipurpose oxbows and
restored floodplains warrant continued monitoring
\parencite{Leberg2024,Schilling2017,ElAgha2020,AbdelDayem2007}.

\subsection{Uncertainty, experimental design, and integrative frameworks}

Several authors identify methodological rather than substantive gaps.
\textcite{Pagliari2021} question whether beta distributions, time series, or new
methods best analyse field-scale, unreplicated paired experiments;
\textcite{Andrus2014} favour directed hypothesis tests over replicate spatial
analyses; and replicated field experiments, matched instrumentation, and improved
sampling designs are recommended for statistical inference
\parencite{Okuda2020,Wamono2016b,Davis2019,Jacquemin2020}. Uncertainty
quantification recurs strongly---stochastic and sensitivity analyses for
parametric uncertainty \parencite{Gates2002,Burkhalter2006}, robust/stochastic
optimisation \parencite{Lei2026}, explicit estimation of prediction accuracy in P
models \parencite{Radcliffe2015}, structural uncertainty via multiple water-quality
models \parencite{Kujawa2020}, and a probabilistic treatment of drainage-water-
management effectiveness across time and scales \parencite{Shedekar2021}.
Standardised evaluation metrics such as the Nash--Sutcliffe coefficient are
proposed \parencite{STONE1995,SAVABI1995,Nazari2021}, independent evaluation
against withheld data and spatially distributed, multi-objective calibration are
recommended \parencite{Schoups2005}, and station-by-station regional trend
analysis is advised \parencite{Kumar2009}. Model transferability---whether a
representative catchment scale applies elsewhere---must be established against
additional watersheds \parencite{Basu2010,Thomas2016}.

A final cross-cutting frontier is methodological integration.
\textcite{Dollinger2015} articulate the most explicit long-range vision:
numerically explicit landscape-scale models that couple the processes governing
ditch services, optimise the spatial arrangement of practices according to
connectivity, and define multi-service maximisation strategies informed by
stakeholder-valued rankings, prerequisite to which is better mapping of ditch
hydraulic, vegetation, and sorption properties \parencite{Dollinger2015,Herzon2008}.
Landscape-scale integrated approaches combining on-farm change with off-site
wetland and riparian restoration are broadly endorsed
\parencite{Blann2009,Goeller2019}, and truly integrated watershed management
requires better information on controlling processes such as
groundwater-head distribution linking topography--geology to the drain fraction
\parencite{Mahmood2023}. Coupled field--watershed models enable water-table-
management scenario analyses not possible with component models used separately
\parencite{Dayyani2010}, frameworks should remain extensible to additional
processes and dynamically calibrated indices \parencite{Basu2010,Tao2025,Verma2014},
and modelling should increasingly guide practice placement and stacking
\parencite{Kröger2013,Tomer2015,Lemke2011,McDowell2021,King2022,Roley2016}.
Adapting drainage design to a changing climate is an explicit priority, tied to
understanding weather--soil--groundwater--discharge interactions under increased
precipitation and to model assessment over longer periods and different climates
\parencite{Deelstra2015,Tuohy2018,Malota2024,Deelstra2014,Deelstra2014b}. Coupled
process representations---water-vapour and heat--water flow in dry soils,
density-driven and hydrobiogeochemical solute transport, and winter runoff and
sediment indicators---round out the frontier
\parencite{Moroizumi2004,Koivusalo1999,Salo2015,Varvaris2018,Augeard2005}.

\subsection{Economics, institutions, and adoption}

A large share of the frontier is socio-economic and institutional. Economic
feasibility and cost--benefit analyses are repeatedly flagged as future
work---for surface amendments and energy savings \parencite{Wamono2016b},
comprehensive short- and long-term subsurface-drainage benefits
\parencite{Guo2025}, paddy-field cost and consumption \parencite{Jia2023},
edge-of-field cost-efficiency \parencite{Mendes2020}, and the opportunity cost of
drainage investment and policies that motivate improvement
\parencite{Wichelns2002,THOMAS1995,Kladivko2005,Saadat2017,Manik2019,Helmers2012}.
Identifying long-term cost savings---such as greater fertiliser-nitrogen-use
efficiency---to offset higher installation costs, together with incentives and
cost-share programs, is recommended \parencite{Castellano2019,Sunohara2015}, and
cost reduction is itself a research target through local manufacture and new
low-cost techniques, materials, and standards \parencite{Datta2002,Yannopoulos2020}.
Detailed management and soil data are emphasised as prerequisites for reliable
inference---field verification of soil information and accurate management records
\parencite{JORJANI1991}, agricultural-management and soil-carbon detail as
watershed-study priorities \parencite{Williams2017}, sound pedological and
geological knowledge \parencite{Jacobsen2007}, and comprehensive databases of
tile-drained land by soil type that can be extrapolated to European soil maps and
folded into water- and nutrient-flux models
\parencite{Hirt2005,Hirt2005b,Hirt2011}.

Institutional and participatory needs are pervasive. Recommendations include
shifting from standardised to flexible, location-specific solutions reflecting
stakeholder preferences, balancing top-down against bottom-up participatory
management across the infrastructure lifecycle, and linking farmers' tacit
knowledge with researchers' explicit knowledge
\parencite{Ritzema2011,Ritzema2016,Ritzema2023}. Capacity development, education,
and policy change to lower adoption barriers are emphasised
\parencite{Ritzema2016,Castellano2019,SHIRMOHAMMADI1995,Kesicka2023b}, along with
governance frameworks, inter-agency coordination, dedicated drainage institutions,
and time-bound reclamation action plans
\parencite{Singh2019c,Qureshi2008,Gopalakrishnan2007,Ritzema2008b,Valipour2014,Quinn2009},
and awareness, subsidy, micro-finance, and participatory operation-and-maintenance
programs \parencite{Satyanarayana2007,Manjunatha2004,BenAissa2013,MCFARLANE1992}.
Continued technical and financial support is deemed essential, and research in
irrigated agriculture is described as ``not yet over''
\parencite{Qureshi2008,Ritzema2007,TeKrony2004}. Implementation, maintenance, and
scaling round out the agenda---large-scale propagation in command areas
\parencite{Karegoudar2019}, sludge removal and careful maintenance of aging
systems \parencite{Yan2013b,AbdElMageed2009,Lamsodis2011}, efficient site
operations and conversion to piped delivery \parencite{Kanber2019}, environmental
monitoring and decision-support built bottom-up from project onset
